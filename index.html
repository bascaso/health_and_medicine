What I'm exploring now

A journey through Astrophysics, AI and healthcare research.

I have a PhD in computational astrophysics and spent 10 more years doing astrophysics research before moving into industry, where I've built ML and AI models and data science and AI teams across e-commerce, travel and construction.

I'm now working towards using AI and Data Science in health and medicine. I've been lucky enough to have two fascinating professional lives: as an astrophysicist and as an AI and data scientist. For my next third one, I am circling back to how data, maths and physics can help us understand our own bodies, detect disease and improve health.

Topics closest to my heart are prevention and detection of cancer, research on Parkinson's and other neurodegenerative diseases, and rare genetic diseases. More generally, using AI to accelerate research in healthcare is a direction I'm exploring. This page is where I'm collecting what I'm learning, the connections I'm finding, and the projects I'm building along the way.

Connecting the dots

What this experience adds up to

In my experience, collaboration between researchers from different disciplines always opens new possibilities to link different domains and same ideas. I call this “connecting the dots.” Personally, across astrophysics research and three industry roles, the same few things keep showing up.

01

Leading research, AI and data science teams end to end

In academia, I led two work packages of 50+ researchers across J-PAS and the Euclid Collaboration. In industry, I co-founded an ML engineering team, later managed data scientists and engineers directly, and today lead three AI teams of 15+ people.

02

Building things, not only studying them

In astrophysics I produced different pieces of research, as for example, a code to simulate Astrophysical surveys (PhotReal) or the Bayesian Cluster Finder, a galaxy cluster detector on optical datasets. In industry, my teams and I have put 30+ models into production, with performance metrics, update pipelines and traceability.

03

Using new AI techniques selectively

I've worked with LLMs and GenAI since 2022, including a help-center in the main platform, an internal synthetic customer to help take decision about new releases and a conversational-commerce agent. Starting with the problem and see whereas the use of GenAI sustantially improves the solution has been a key learning.

04

Connecting technical work to the wider organisation

My work has also included AI strategy, whitepapers, internal education, hackathons and cross-functional initiatives. Academia had its own version of this: international collaborations, 50+ conferences, 70+ publications and outreach.

05

Keeping rigor as a habit

In academia, spending years of peer review create rigurosity a habit. This has been extrapolated to industry, as I have proven that it translates into long-term value. Dealing with uncertainty, incomplete datasets and having the ability to find solutions and explain them to any person is crucial in any discipline.

I have spent a long time moving between maths, physics, research, machine learning, engineering, products and teams. Health and medicine add a new domain with their own science, language, constraints and regulatory discipline. However, many of the underlying problems feel familiar: incomplete signals, uncertainty, complex systems and the need to work across disciplines.

What comes next

For now, I'm enjoying connecting a new set of dots.

I know I'm coming to health and medicine from the outside, and there is a great deal I still need to learn about the domain. At the same time, I have accumulated an unusual mix of experience across research, maths and physics, production ML, AI, engineering and team leadership. I'm curious to see what that combination can become when applied seriously to health.

So I'm preparing for the transition in the way that suits me best: learning, building, reading, talking to people from the field and having fun with the problems. I trust the experience I've built over the years, and I'm happy to let the next step emerge from the work.
